Title: About

I'm from Brooklyn. When I was a kid, my family moved to the outer suburbs of Atlanta, and I hated it. I couldn't go anywhere unless my parents drove me, so for everyone I knew, a car at sixteen meant freedom. In my last term of high school, seven students died. Six of them died in car crashes, and there were always grief counsellors at the school. When we visited family back in New York, I had the freedom of the city. It took me years to see how much of the difference was the places themselves.

Since then I've lived in Orlando, Washington DC, Santiago de Chile, Montréal, Sydney, and Cambridge, and I keep comparing them. New York is still my favourite; it has everything. Santiago is dense and walkable, DC always has something going on, and Montréal is affordable and easy to cycle. My wife's family lives in Sydney, and I've spent several long stretches there.

I studied international affairs and worked at the UN and in a library before moving into data science, then data engineering. Today I lead software and data teams. From 2022 to 2026 I also sat on Girton Parish Council's planning committee. I joined because Cambridge is growing fast and needs more homes, and I didn't want parking requirements to stand in the way of building them. I want them to be homes the people who grew up here can afford.

I don't own a car. I get around mostly by bike, and by bus when my three kids are with me, with a car club car about once a month. To me, how we build places is a question of fairness, and of looking after the world we've been given: compact, walkable places use less land and fewer resources.

I'm not a planner. I read, watch, and listen to a lot about cities, and I'm working towards doing data, software, or research work on transport, housing, and placemaking. My project recording every bus in Sydney is part of that.

I'm working out loud about why some places work.
