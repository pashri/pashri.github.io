Title: About

I'm from Brooklyn. When I was a kid, my family moved to the outer suburbs of Atlanta, and I hated it. For years I couldn't explain why. It took me a long time to see that much of the answer was the place itself.

Since then I've lived in Orlando, Washington DC, Santiago de Chile, Montréal, Sydney, and Cambridge, and I keep comparing them. Why are some places easy and pleasant to live in, and others not? And how do we build the good kind so that ordinary people can afford to live there?

I studied international affairs and worked at the UN and in a library before moving into data science, then data engineering. Today I lead software and data teams. From 2022 to 2026 I also sat on Girton Parish Council's planning committee. I joined because Cambridge is growing fast and needs more homes, and I didn't want parking requirements to stand in the way of building them. I want the people who grew up here to be able to afford to stay.

I don't own a car. I get around mostly by bike, and by bus when my three kids are with me, with a car club car about once a month. To me, how we build places is a question of fairness, and of looking after the world we've been given: compact, walkable places use less land and fewer resources.

I'm not a planner. I read, watch, and listen to a lot about cities, and I'm working my way towards a career in urbanism. These notes are where I think things through. My project recording every bus in Sydney is part of that.
